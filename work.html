02
Independent projectTransportation · Spatial analytics

UDOT SafeCorridor Intelligence

An independent exposure-adjusted roadway-safety prioritization and decision-support system built from public Utah transportation data.

449,804crash records processed
96.5%eligible crash-to-AADT match rate
519five-mile corridors tested
45supported corridor clusters

Case addressed

  • Raw severe-crash counts do not account for how much traffic exposure a roadway carries.
  • The project asks which corridors show unusually high fatal and serious-injury burden after exposure adjustment.
  • It also separates prioritization signals from causal claims and documents limitations explicitly.

What I built

  • Python ingestion from public UDOT sources with schema normalization and Parquet persistence.
  • Spatial route matching, linear referencing, five-mile corridor construction, and exposure adjustment.
  • Observed-vs-expected screening with Poisson uncertainty and Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate control.
  • A Streamlit dashboard with statewide exploration, priority corridor screening, drill-down, and methodology views.

Technology

  • Python, SQL, DuckDB, Pandas, GeoPandas, Shapely, SciPy, Parquet, ArcGIS REST.
  • Streamlit, Altair, PyDeck, and GitHub Actions.
  • Bronze → Silver → Gold analytical transformations with automated QA outputs.

What changed

  • Crash location, exposure, geometry, uncertainty, and corridor consolidation became one reproducible analytical chain.
  • The workflow achieved a 99.0% valid spatial route-match rate and 0.29 m median snap distance.
  • A weekly GitHub Actions pipeline can refresh the published analytical outputs automatically.

05
Portfolio projectHealthcare analytics · Tableau

Breast Cancer Survival + Clinical Pattern Analysis

A descriptive Tableau analysis of 4,024 breast-cancer patient records, built to move beyond a reassuring overall outcome rate and show where recorded outcomes diverge across disease severity and clinical characteristics.

4,024patient records analyzed
84.7%alive at last recorded follow-up
16clinical and demographic fields

Case addressed

  • An overall survival headline can hide meaningful differences across cancer stage, lymph-node involvement, tumor grade, tumor size, and hormone-receptor status.
  • The challenge was to turn a multi-variable clinical dataset into a connected analytical story rather than a collection of unrelated charts.
  • The analysis needed to remain descriptive and avoid implying causal or population-level medical conclusions.

What I built

  • Structured a three-part Tableau story: Patient & Outcomes Overview, Clinical Factors & Outcomes, and Patient Profile & Disease Characteristics.
  • Built KPI cards, stage and outcome views, receptor-status analysis, a stage × tumor-grade heatmap, tumor-size vs positive-node scatter analysis, and patient-profile views.
  • Reworked the narrative around Hook → Insight → So-What so the story begins with the 84.7% recorded-alive headline and then drills into where outcomes diverge.

Technology

  • Tableau, exploratory data analysis, descriptive statistics, visual analytics, and data storytelling.
  • Fields included cancer stage, N stage, tumor grade, tumor size, positive regional lymph nodes, ER/PR status, age, marital status, survival months, and recorded vital status.
  • Interactive visual design focused on comparison, drill-down, and clinically meaningful segmentation.

What the analysis surfaced

  • The overall recorded-alive rate was 84.7%, but the story showed that the headline should not be treated as a single summary score for every subgroup.
  • Greater disease severity and lymph-node involvement were associated with visibly poorer recorded outcomes in the supplied dataset.
  • The final narrative directs the viewer from the headline outcome into the clinical segments where the differences become most important.

Descriptive analysis of the supplied dataset for portfolio and educational use. It does not establish causality, population incidence, diagnosis, prognosis, or clinical guidance.
